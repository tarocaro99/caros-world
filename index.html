This is where the experiments live. Nothing in this tab is polished, and that's on purpose. I treat every project like a prototype: try the thing I don't know how to do yet, ship a rough version, and learn from whatever breaks.

"Fail-Safe" is how I think. A failed experiment is safe as long as it teaches me something I can carry into the next build, and "Absurd Optimism" is what keeps me trying the next one. These pieces are hand-drawn and hand-edited, no AI, made just to see if I could. Seeing where my skills stop is how I figure out where to push next.

I'm Caroline Chen, a designer with a background in economics, which basically means I like figuring out why people do irrational things on screens.

Growing up near San Francisco, I spent a lot of time wandering Chinatown, mostly drawn to the subtle layout details in storefronts and the daily routines of people passing through. That early curiosity about how people naturally navigate physical spaces is really what pulled me toward design in the first place.

I recently wrapped up at UC Davis with a BA in Design and a BS in Managerial Economics. These days, I focus on user experience and ethnographic fieldwork. I like getting out into real spaces to observe actual human habits, then figuring out how those behaviors translate into functional digital systems. A lot of my work digs into themes around productivity culture, habit loops, and the subtle social irony built into the tools we use every day. On the side, I currently help with GTM strategy, running Meta ads and X campaigns.

Admittedly, I'm an iPad kid. It's my go-to digital sketchbook for pulling together quick graphic assets and experimenting with textures. But I don't confine myself strictly to digital work, either. My projects hop around between user interfaces, field research, and physical print design.

A lot of my creative energy comes from deep-diving into the hyperpop and EDM scenes. The raw stimulation and chaotic energy of the music push me to take bigger visual risks and explore bolder ideas.

Going forward, I just want to decode real human habits, strip away the noise, and turn them into thoughtful products with a great team. I'm naturally drawn to new tech, unfamiliar ideas, and learning from how other people work. The final output always matters, but for me, the shift in perspective and knowledge gained during the build is where the real value lives.
